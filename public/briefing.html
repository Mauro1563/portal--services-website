Multi‑tenant architecture

The tenant is the security and data‑ownership boundary. One company can manage many clients; one client, several contracts; one contract, several locations. One tenant’s data is never authority for another.

Identity & access

People & Access — identity separated from authority

The key architectural distinction: the operational role describes the work; administrative authority is controlled separately. They do not mix.

= identity

Person

The real person. Canonical identity core, never duplicated per contract, location or role.

= participation

UserAccount

Account that participates in the system. Optional — a worker can exist with no login.

= relationship

Employment

Person ↔ Tenant employment relationship. Not a service contract.

= placement

Assignment

Real operational placement: contract + location (via active link) + operational role.

Person ≠ UserAccount ≠ Firebase Auth  ·  OperationalRole ≠ Administrative Authority

The operational role describes the function performed. Administrative access is controlled separately — today by the current authority model (systemRole); in the future, by fine‑grained RBAC.

Multilingual communication

One message, everyone in their language

An operative writes in Spanish → the original message is preserved → translation layer → the manager reads it in English. The original message and traceability are retained. AI does not modify official records without control.

AI authority model

AI operates under security, never above it

An authority ladder: AI never sits above the security architecture. Security controls AI; AI does not bypass permissions. Each level is enabled under current policy and authority.

AI → Tenant Scope → Authorization → Policy → Human approval where required → Server Capability → Audit
